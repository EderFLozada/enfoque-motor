NASCAR México Series Closed The 2026 Season

Eder Lozada 9 November 2026
  • Rubén García Jr clinched his sixth NASCAR México Series championship
  • Gerardo ‘Chispa’ Rodríguez finished his rookie season on the top of the Challenge division.

The NASCAR México Series finale, ran at the Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez, defined the new champions from the major series and the Challenge division.

While the spotlight was on the title battles, Julio Rejón stepped out to claim his first win of the season.

Rubén García Jr overtook Xavi Razo on the standings and finished his job to take his sixth NASCAR México Championship. The 30 year old driver has reached the top of the 2015, 2018, 2019, 2022, 2024 and now 2026 seasons.

Gerardo ‘Chispa’ Rodríguez had and up-and-down performance but still kept the championship lead throughout the 132 laps around the 1.6 kilometer (1 mile) oval.

Intensity

The 12th Round, named ‘La Gran FInal 150’, started with a big splash: championship leader Xavi Razo led the field into the green flag; Rubén García Jr didn’t waste any time and attacked Razo heading into the first corner. The No. 7 car driver lifted to avoid contact and lost ground.

On lap 3 the first yellow flag was waved: Rubén Rovelo, who entered the race 3rd on the standings, had contact with car No. 9 and went off to the wall. The No. 28 car suffered two punctures, damage and lost his chance to fight for the drivers championship.

On the restart Razo and García Jr resumed their fight. This time the No.7 machine prevailed over No. 88. Razo’s lead ended quickly as Julio Rejón took over the top spot and went on to win Stage 1.

García Jr passed Razo to finish 3rd on the lap 60 cut.

‘Chispa’ Rodríguez grabbed pole position on the Challenge division. With the intense start the Alessandros Racing driver opted for a conservative approach and let his rivals took over the lead.

Emiliano Richards climbed to the top spot and claimed the Stage win followed by Mateo Girón and ‘Chispa’ Rodríguez.

Sunset

The final segment of the NASCAR México Series season was characterized by the battle for the win between García Jr and Julio Rejón; also by the lack of natural light —Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez doesn’t have lights— reason why race control opted for a timed finish instead of the original 150 laps distance.

Two early yellow flags allowed all teams to make their mandatory fuel pit stop. Lap 68 registered a multiple-car pile up that affected cars No. 9, 13, 39, 75, 77 and 89.

García Jr restarted 1st and stood on top over 45 laps while keeping the car No. 55 close in his mirror until the inevitable pass with 16 laps to go. Rejón grabbed his only win of the season.

“Unbelievable, what a year for my team-owner Jordi Vidal, my GGG Racing Team and for me as a driver! With his leadership he kept us all race motivated, always with a plan and that was it, as our season went on,” said Julio Rejón after his win.

After the last restart García Jr held position and brought his stock No. 88 home in 2nd place, earning enough points to claim his sixth championship in the series.

“Very few times I’ve raced with that intensity in my career. From the race start I gave it my all to keep myself in the front, earning the maximum points I could,” claimed Rubén García Jr after the race and also took time to recognize Razo’s performance through the season.

Emiliano Richards led most of the second half of ‘La Gran Final 150’ until the last restart when he got involved in ‘bumping & banging’ with other drivers and lost ground on the battle for the win.

Andrik Dimayuga attempted to surprise his rivals on the last laps to get to Victory Lane but ‘Chispa’ Rodríguez reacted to get back in front of his rivals and grab his second win of the season. Throughout the race the rookie from the No. 27 machine kep the lead of the championship.

“It’s a testament of what our season was like: from a low to a high. It took us a while to build race pace,” admitted the newly crowned Challenge champion and added: “Starting the race with a big point gap helped us a lot”.

Precise

Trucks México Series joined NASCAR México on their visit to Mexico City. The pick-ups took their green flag around the 1 mile oval at noon.

Stage 1 became a relentless battle for the lead. Poleman Carlos Novelo led the field on the first laps and exchanged the top-spot with Roberto Espinosa, Jan Krüll —main championship rivals — and Santiago Cruz during the first 30 laps.

Truck No.7 showed speed while championship leader Espinosa struggled to keep the pace with the leaders.

Krüll studied his rivals and mounted his attacks with precision. The driver from ANVI Motorsports grabbed Stage 1 win with a late pass to Cruz and earned 5 points to cut Espinosa lead.

The final stage of the season was dominated by Santiago Cruz, who led most of the way. Krüll and Espinosa had to climb through the field after making their mandatory tire-change during the Stage-break.

A yellow flag on lap 59 changed the outcome of the race and the drivers championship: on the restart Espinosa struggled to keep the pace up with the leader. While fighting to maintain spots he made contact with truck No. 51 on the ext of turn 4, was sent to the outside wall and ended his season with a DNF.

With the championship defined in his favor, Krüll went on to attack for the lead on the final restart. The truck No. 7 grabbed the lead to seal his season on a high.

“It was the most difficult race I’ve ever had, the most challenging one. This place decided it all and we took the ‘Chase’ points lead, the championship, which we wanted since the start of the year,” said a tired Jan Philipp Krüll after the race.
